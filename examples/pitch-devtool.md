# Example: `/steve pitch`

**The ask.** "I've been building this for 8 months and I still can't explain it to people at meetups."

**Input:** a local-first sync engine for JavaScript apps, and a tagline nobody understands.

---

**Mode:** pitch · TaskSync, local-first sync for JS apps

*Working from your paragraph only — I have not seen the repo, the site or the API.*

**The one thing.** "Your work is wherever you are — the other device, the tunnel, the dead wifi — and you never think about it."

**Headline.** Google Docs sync for any app. One npm install.
Runners-up: "Close the laptop, open your phone, it's all still there." — the best picture of the three, but it describes a moment, not a thing a stranger can go install. "Stop building sync twice." — the right enemy, and every engineer nods, but there is nothing to see.

**Three things.**
1. Close the laptop mid-sentence. Open your phone. The cursor is where you left it.
2. Two people type into the same thing at once and neither one loses a word.
3. The month you were going to spend building sync — you keep it.

**The demo moment.** Laptop and phone side by side, phone in airplane mode. Type on both. Turn the wifi back on. → the room says: "wait, that's it?"

**Before → after.**
Before: "TaskSync: the modern state synchronization layer for reactive applications."
After: "TaskSync gives your app Google Docs sync. Wrap your state — close the laptop, open your phone, it's all there. Works with no signal. React, Vue, Svelte."

CRDTs and optional end-to-end encryption are not in that sentence on purpose. That is how it works, and nobody buys the how. Second paragraph, for the engineer already leaning in.

**The name.** "TaskSync" says you sync tasks, so the stranger pictures a to-do app before you finish your sentence — and then spends the rest of it correcting the picture. You built infrastructure. Short, sayable, and about the moment, not the payload. Change it now; you will be saying it for ten years.

*The headline and the name are taste, not measurement. Say all three lines out loud at the next meetup and keep whichever one gets a question instead of a nod.*

**What Steve would say.** *(imagined)* "You wrote a tagline for your sync engine. Write one for the guy who closes his laptop and picks up his phone."
