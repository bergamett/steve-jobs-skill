# Example: `/steve email`

**The ask.** A customer offers real money for a platform port the developer does not want to build.

**Input:** a feature-request email, a solo developer, and $1,400 a year on the table.

---

**Mode:** email · Windows, SSO, twelve seats

**The answer.** No. You are not losing a customer here, because you never had one — twelve Windows seats is not $1,400 on the table, it is $1,400 you would have to build for six months to collect, once, from one team. "Not yet" is the lie in this situation: it costs them a year of waiting for something you are not going to build, and it costs you the year too.

**Reply (short).**
> Thanks — glad the app is working for you. No Windows version, and no SSO. There is one of me, and the Mac app is the only thing I can make great.
> Don't wait on me. If there is a Windows tool that does the job, take it, and if your team ever lands on Macs I'm here.

**Reply (long).** *(you will be asked this every month — write it once, put it on the site, link it)*
> I am not building a Windows version, and I am not building SSO. Here is the reasoning, so you can plan around it.
> 1. There is one of me. Windows is six months of my year, and SSO is not a weekend after that.
> 2. Twelve seats is $1,400 a year. Six months of my time needs about twenty teams your size before it pays for itself, and twenty teams are not asking.
> 3. Those six months are six months the Mac app stops getting better — and the Mac app is the reason you wrote to me.
> Mac only, this year and next. If you need Windows now, go get it. I would rather you had a tool that works than a promise from me.

**What it costs.** $1,400 a year you will not book, a team that buys something else this month and probably stays bought, and the two or three referrals they would have sent you.

*The arithmetic is measurement; walking away from the money is taste. If twenty teams send you this same email in a quarter, that is data and it beats every line above — so count them in a file, don't count them from memory.*
