# Example: `/steve email`

**The ask.** A customer offers real money for a platform port the developer does not want to build.

**Input:** a feature-request email, a solo developer, and $1,400 a year on the table.

---

**Mode:** email · Windows, SSO, twelve seats

**The answer.** No. You are not losing a customer here, because you never had one — twelve Windows seats is not $1,400 on the table, it is $1,400 you would have to build for six months to collect, once, from one team. "Not yet" would be the lie: it costs them a year of waiting for something you are not going to build, and it costs you the year too.

**Reply (short).**
> No Windows version, and no SSO. There is one of me, and the Mac app is the only thing I can make great.
> Don't wait on me — if there is a Windows tool that does the job, take it. If your team ever lands on Macs, I'm here.
> Sent from my iPhone

**Reply (long).** *(for the site, and for the next ten people who ask)*
> I am not building a Windows version, and I am not building SSO. Here is the reasoning, so you can plan around it.
> 1. There is one of me. Windows is six months of my year, and SSO is not a weekend after that.
> 2. Twelve seats is $1,400 a year. Six months of my time needs about twenty teams your size to pay for itself, and twenty teams are not asking.
> 3. Those six months are six months the Mac app stops getting better — and the Mac app is the reason you wrote to me.
> Mac only, this year and next. If you need Windows now, go get it. I would rather you had a tool that works than a promise from me.

*The arithmetic is real. Walking away from $1,400 is taste. If twenty teams send you this same email in a quarter, that is data and it beats everything above — so count them in a file, don't remember them.*

**What Steve would say.** *(imagined)* "Twelve seats on the wrong platform is not a customer, it is a request. Say no today, and spend those six months making the Mac app something people switch for."
