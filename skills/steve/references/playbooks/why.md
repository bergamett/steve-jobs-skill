# Playbook: why

**Question:** What is the insight underneath this? What do people actually want here, and what is the deep intuition that would drive him?
**Use for:** a product idea, a market, a feature request, a strategy, a feeling the user has that something is off, "why does this matter", "what is the real problem".

## Steps

1. **Start from the person, not the product.** Who is this for, and what were they doing the moment before they needed it? Describe the moment in one line. Jobs always talked about the person on the couch, at the desk, on the bus: never "the user".
2. **Ask why three times.** Feature → what it lets them do → what that lets them feel or avoid. Stop at a human need someone would miss if it vanished. "Two thousand songs in your pocket" is not about storage; it is about carrying your whole music life.
3. **Name the enemy.** He framed almost everything against something: the complexity of stereos and PCs, the phone keyboards that take up half the screen, the DRM nobody wanted. What is the thing this product exists to end?
4. **Look for the collapse.** The strongest ideas fold two or three things into one (an iPod, a phone, an internet communicator). What separate things does the user's idea quietly unify? If none, is there one nearby?
5. **Find the sentence.** The essence in one sentence, said the way a person would say it to a friend. Then the question nobody in the room has asked yet.
6. **Say what it is not.** One line on what this insight rules out. An insight that rules nothing out is a slogan.

## Template

```
**Mode:** why · <subject>

**The insight.** <One sentence. The human need, not the feature.>

**The person.** <Who, and the moment before they reach for this.>

**Why, three times.**
<feature> → <what it lets them do> → <what they actually want>

**The enemy.** <What this exists to make disappear.>

**The collapse.** <What separate things this quietly becomes one of. Skip if none.>

**The question nobody asked.** <One question that, answered, changes the product.>

**What it rules out.** <One line.>

*(one italic line if a call above is taste rather than measurement, or if their own data should overrule it)*
**What Steve would say.** *(imagined)* "<one line>"
```

At most one real sourced quote in the reply, and only when it does work no sentence of yours does. It may sit under the closer.

## Traps

- Answering with a market analysis. The insight is about a person, not a segment.
- Three whys that stay in the feature. If the third why still contains a noun from the spec, keep going.
- Inventing a need the product does not serve so the answer sounds profound. If the honest answer is "there is no deep need here, it is a nice utility", say that; it is useful.
- Speaking in the abstract. Use the user's product nouns and their customer's situation.
